# PRD: Rich Profiles

**Tier:** 2 &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Status:** Proposed &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Owner:** TBD

## Problem
The profile page today is functional but flat: avatar, bio, articles. Visitors can't tell when the user joined, who they follow, what their best work is, or how to reach them off-platform. For a blogging app where the author *is* the brand, that's a missed opportunity. A few low-cost additions — a pinned article, social links, follower counts, a member-since date — turn the profile from a content list into something that actually identifies the person.

## Goals
- Profile shows: pinned article (author's choice), social links, follower/following counts, member-since date.
- The author can pin/unpin one of their own articles from the article detail page.
- Settings page grows a "Social links" section.

## Non-Goals
- Profile background images, custom themes, or layout choices.
- Verified-author badges or credentials.
- A second pinned article. One is the cap; that's the discipline.
- Editing follower/following counts (they're derived).

## User Stories
- As a visitor, I land on a profile and see the user's pinned article highlighted at the top.
- As a visitor, I see "Following 12 · 47 followers · Joined March 2024" near the bio.
- As an author, I click "Pin to profile" on my own article's detail page; it now appears at the top of my profile.
- As an author, I add my Twitter, GitHub, and personal website URLs in settings; they render as small icons on my profile.
- As an author, I unpin by clicking again — no confirmation dialog needed.

## UX Sketch
Profile page header (`templates/accounts/profile.html` or wherever the profile body lives in `articles/views.py::profile_view`) gets a refresh: pinned article block above the article-tab strip, follower/following row inline with the bio, social-link icons in a horizontal strip under the username. Settings page grows a section with three or four URL inputs. The "Pin to profile" action lives on the article detail page (author-only button), `hx-post` to `/article/<slug>/pin`, swaps a confirmation toast and updates the button label.

## Data Model Changes
- `User.pinned_article = ForeignKey("articles.Article", null=True, blank=True, on_delete=models.SET_NULL, related_name="+")`.
- `User.social_links = JSONField(default=dict)` — keys are well-known platforms (`twitter`, `github`, `mastodon`, `website`), values are URLs. Validated against an allowlist in the form.
- `User.date_joined` already exists on `AbstractUser` — wire it up in templates as "member-since".
- Follower/following counts are derived (`User.followers.count()`, reverse query for following) — no schema change.

## Key Technical Decisions
- **JSONField over a `SocialLink` table.** Fixed allowlist (4–6 platforms), bounded size, no need to query across users by platform. JSON is simpler and the validation is a 10-line form clean.
- **Pin via FK to Article**, not a Boolean on Article. A user pins exactly one article; the FK enforces that with no extra checking. `on_delete=SET_NULL` handles the article-deleted case cleanly.
- **No icon library.** Inline SVG strings in the template for the 4–6 known platforms. Alternative considered: `django-fontawesome` — overkill for 6 icons.

## Risks & Open Questions
- **Counts in the header.** Showing follower count on every profile view is one COUNT query each. With `select_related` plus an annotation, fine — but worth checking the SQL in dev.
- **Pinned-article integrity.** The pinned article must belong to the user. Enforce in the view, not just the template.
- **Open question:** social-link allowlist — Twitter, GitHub, Mastodon, personal website is the obvious starting set. LinkedIn? Bluesky? Decide at planning, not later.

## Success Metrics
- Profile page renders in <120ms (p95) with the new fields.
- 100% coverage on the new pin/unpin view and social-link form validation.
- E2e baseline stays green.

## Rough Effort
M — schema additions on User, settings form change, profile template restructure, new pin view. No migration risk to existing data; all fields are nullable or default-empty.

## Dependencies
- None. Pairs naturally with **20.03 Article Reactions** (a "top-clapped" sidebar on the profile is one obvious extension) and with the existing follow flow.
