# PRD: Reading Time and View Counts

**Tier:** 3 &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Status:** Proposed &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Owner:** TBD

## Problem
Article cards on the home and tag feeds give the reader no signal about length or popularity. A 300-word quick-take and a 4,000-word essay look identical, and there's no way to tell which articles other readers have found valuable. Every modern blogging surface (Medium, Substack, dev.to) shows estimated reading time and some form of engagement counter; their absence makes our cards feel sparse and unhelpful.

## Goals
- Every article card shows an estimated read time (e.g. `4 min read`).
- Article detail pages show a view count.
- View counts are deduplicated so a single reader refreshing doesn't inflate the count.
- Both numbers are cheap to render — no extra query per card on the feed page.

## Non-Goals
- Per-article analytics dashboards for authors (Tier 4 follow-up).
- Time-on-page / scroll-depth tracking. View = "saw the article detail page" only.
- Showing view counts on cards (only on detail pages, to keep feed UI clean and to avoid coordinating writes from list pages).

## User Stories
- As a reader skimming the home feed, I can see "5 min read" on each card so I can pick something I have time for.
- As a reader on an article page, I can see "1,247 views" so I know whether it's been popular.
- As an author, I can see the same view count on my own articles to know if anyone's reading.
- As an operator, I am confident the view counter doesn't blow up the database under load.

## UX Sketch
**Read time:** computed from `Article.content` word count divided by 265 wpm (industry-standard estimate), surfaced as a templatetag filter `{{ article.content|read_time }}` returning a string like `"4 min read"`. Rendered on every card in `partials/article_preview.html` and on the article detail header. Pure function — no DB hit, no migration.

**View count:** rendered in the article detail meta line next to the favorite count. The article detail view increments the counter as a side effect, with dedupe (see below).

## Data Model Changes
- `Article.view_count: PositiveIntegerField(default=0, db_index=False)` — denormalized counter, incremented in-place via `F("view_count") + 1` to avoid read-modify-write races.
- **No** new `ArticleView` table for v1. See decision below.
- Optional: `Article.read_time_cached: PositiveSmallIntegerField(null=True)` if profiling shows the templatetag is hot — defer until measured.

## Key Technical Decisions
- **Counter column over event log.** A precise `ArticleView(user_or_session, article, viewed_at)` table gives perfect analytics but multiplies article-detail writes by every page view, and the index is huge. The aggregated counter is one `UPDATE` per dedupe-pass and zero rows added. Tradeoff explicitly accepted: we lose per-day breakdowns and per-reader history. The day we want those, we add the event table alongside the counter, not in place of it.
- **Dedupe via session.** The article-detail view stores viewed article IDs in `request.session["viewed_articles"]` (a small bounded LRU, e.g. last 200 ids). If the article ID is in the set, skip the increment; otherwise increment and add. Anonymous users get session-scoped dedupe; logged-in users get the same with their session, which is good enough for v1.
- **Increment off the request hot path** via Django's `transaction.on_commit` so a rendering error doesn't bump the count and the counter write doesn't block the response.
- Read time uses **265 wpm** (Medium's published figure). Round up; show `< 1 min read` floor.

## Risks & Open Questions
- **Risk:** Bot/crawler traffic inflates counts. Mitigation: skip increment when `request.user.is_authenticated is False` and the User-Agent matches a known-bot regex; revisit if the noise is bad.
- **Risk:** Author refreshing their own draft repeatedly. Mitigation: never increment when `request.user == article.author`.
- **Open question:** Show view count to logged-out readers, or only to logged-in? Recommendation: show to everyone — it's social proof.

## Success Metrics
- Read time appears on 100% of article cards in e2e tests.
- View count increments exactly once per (session, article) pair in unit tests.
- p95 article-detail latency unchanged (within 5%) post-launch.

## Rough Effort
S — one templatetag filter, one migration (single `IntegerField`), small change to `article_detail_view`, session helper. No new dependencies.

## Dependencies
- None. This is a self-contained polish PRD — does not depend on any Tier 1/2 work, though it stacks naturally with **20.04 Search** (cards become richer surfaces for ranked results).
